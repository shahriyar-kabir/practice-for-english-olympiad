# Wizpiko original practice collection

This collection replaces the previous age-category practice wording and question bank. It contains seven skill paths, 210 newly written contextual crossword clues and 70 newly written multiple-choice questions. All starting points remain open at every age. These are learning activities rather than an exam or a proficiency certification.

Puzzle play is untimed. Multiple-choice rounds contain five questions selected from each path’s authored collection; additional practice may revisit concepts. A time or deadline mentioned inside an English question is part of its reading scenario, never a countdown imposed on the learner.

Existing category IDs are retained internally for saved-record compatibility. The public names and descriptions below replace the old category labels.

| Public path | Skill description | Internal compatibility ID |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Everyday Explorer | Read useful signs and make sense of daily tasks. | `kids` |

| Conversation Corner | Practise clear requests, thoughtful replies and helpful updates. | `juniors` |
| Meaning Makers | Read carefully, weigh word choices and check what evidence supports. | `high-flyers` |
| Idea Builders | Organise reasons, compare proposals and shape useful ideas. | `trailblazers` |
| Clear Communicators | Write focused messages and communicate with clarity and care. | `seniors` |

### Multiple-choice questions and answer key

#### 1. I have a cup. I want water. What can I say?

A. Good night.
B. I would like a hat.
C. Close the bag.
D. Water, please.

**Answer:** Water, please.

**Explanation:** “Water, please” asks politely for the drink you want.

#### 2. Read: “Put the pen in the bag.” Where does the pen go?

A. By the tap
B. Under the bed
C. In the bag
D. On the hat

**Answer:** In the bag

**Explanation:** The words “in the bag” tell you where to put the pen.

#### 3. Choose the word that completes the message: “My name ___ Nila.”

A. be
B. is
C. are
D. am

**Answer:** is

**Explanation:** Use “is” after “My name”: “My name is Nila.”

#### 4. A sign says “OPEN”. You want to enter. What does the sign tell you?

A. You may go in.
B. The shop has moved.
C. The shop is closed.
D. You must go home.

**Answer:** You may go in.

**Explanation:** “Open” tells you the place is ready for people to enter.

#### 5. Read: “I can see two hats.” How many hats are there?

A. One
B. Three
C. Four
D. Two

**Answer:** Two

**Explanation:** The sentence gives the number directly: two hats.

#### 6. Choose the sentence with a capital letter at the start and a full stop at the end.

A. I have a bag
B. i have a bag
C. I have a bag.
D. i have a bag.

**Answer:** I have a bag.

**Explanation:** A complete written sentence starts with a capital letter and ends here with a full stop.

#### 7. Read: “The cup is on the desk.” Which thing is on the desk?

A. The towel
B. The cup
C. The coat
D. The key

**Answer:** The cup

**Explanation:** “The cup” is the thing placed on the desk.

#### 8. You finish using a pen that a friend lent you. What can you say when you give it back?

A. Thank you.
B. My name is a pen.
C. Good morning, bed.
D. I want to sleep.

**Answer:** Thank you.

**Explanation:** “Thank you” shows that you appreciate your friend lending the pen.

#### 9. Choose the word that begins with the same letter as “bag”.

A. cup
B. hat
C. pen
D. bed

**Answer:** bed

**Explanation:** Both “bag” and “bed” begin with the letter b.

#### 10. Read: “Take a coat. It is cold outside.” What should you take?

A. A fork
B. A lid
C. A coat
D. A plate

**Answer:** A coat

**Explanation:** The instruction says “Take a coat” to help you stay warm.

### Multiple-choice questions and answer key

#### 1. A notice says: “Bread ready at 10. Please queue here.” What should a customer do while waiting?

A. Walk straight into the kitchen.
B. Wait at the railway platform.
C. Leave money on the pavement.
D. Stand in the line at the marked place.

**Answer:** Stand in the line at the marked place.

**Explanation:** The instruction “Please queue here” asks customers to line up where indicated.

#### 2. Yesterday I ___ a postcard to my friend. Choose the correct word.

A. sending
B. sends
C. sent
D. send

**Answer:** sent

**Explanation:** “Yesterday” places the action in the past, so use “sent”.

#### 3. Lena borrows a basket from Amir. What does “borrows” tell us?

A. She is selling the basket.
B. She plans to give the basket back.
C. She has bought the basket.
D. She has thrown the basket away.

**Answer:** She plans to give the basket back.

**Explanation:** Borrowing means using something that belongs to someone else and returning it.

#### 4. Your bus stop is beside the bakery. Which direction is the clearest?

A. Wait next to the bakery.
B. Wait somewhere nice.
C. Go very far.
D. Find a thing outside.

**Answer:** Wait next to the bakery.

**Explanation:** “Next to the bakery” gives a specific, useful location.

#### 5. Read: “Bring a cup and a plate. Drinks are provided.” What do you need to bring?

A. A bottle of juice only
B. A cup and a recipe
C. A plate and a stamp
D. A cup and a plate

**Answer:** A cup and a plate

**Explanation:** The notice lists a cup and a plate; it says the drinks will be provided.

#### 6. A recipe says: “Wash the beans before cooking them.” What does it tell you to do before cooking?

A. Put the beans on a plate.
B. Eat the beans.
C. Wash the beans.
D. Serve the cooked beans.

**Answer:** Wash the beans.

**Explanation:** “Before” shows the order: wash first, then cook.

#### 7. You cannot reach a packet on a high shelf. Which request fits?

A. Where does this bus stop?
B. Could you pass me that packet, please?
C. Could you move the empty shelf, please?
D. May I borrow your basket instead?

**Answer:** Could you pass me that packet, please?

**Explanation:** The request names the item and asks politely for the help needed.

#### 8. Choose the complete sentence.

A. The shop closes at six.
B. The shop at six.
C. Closes at the.
D. At six the.

**Answer:** The shop closes at six.

**Explanation:** “The shop closes at six” tells us who or what, the action, and when.

#### 9. A calendar reminder reads: “Dentist, Tuesday, 3 pm.” What is the reminder about?

A. A recipe
B. A shop receipt
C. A postcard picture
D. An appointment

**Answer:** An appointment

**Explanation:** A dentist visit scheduled for a date and time is an appointment.

#### 10. You want to keep a receipt because you may need to return a purchase. Which message explains that?

A. Please put the bread in a bag.
B. When does the shop open tomorrow?
C. Please give me a receipt for this purchase.
D. Could you tell me where the exit is?

**Answer:** Please give me a receipt for this purchase.

**Explanation:** A receipt records the purchase and may be needed when returning it.

### Multiple-choice questions and answer key

#### 1. Read: “Mina put the seed tray beside the window. She wanted the young plants to get more light.” Why did she move the tray?

A. To hide the tray from visitors.
B. To keep the plants in darkness.
C. To make space for a television.
D. To give the plants more light.

**Answer:** To give the plants more light.

**Explanation:** The second sentence states her reason: she wanted the plants to get more light.

#### 2. Read: “The parcel felt light, but it made a soft rattle when Jo moved it.” Which detail describes a sound?

A. It felt light.
B. Jo moved it.
C. It made a soft rattle.
D. It was a parcel.

**Answer:** It made a soft rattle.

**Explanation:** A rattle is a sound; the other details describe the object, its weight or an action.

#### 3. A story begins at a busy station and ends in a quiet village. Which statement is supported?

A. Every scene takes place in one room.
B. The setting changes during the story.
C. Every character lives at the station.
D. The story gives no information about places.

**Answer:** The setting changes during the story.

**Explanation:** The locations given at the beginning and end are different, so the setting changes.

#### 4. Read: “First, Nuri drew the shape. Next, he cut it out. Finally, he painted it.” What did Nuri do immediately before painting?

A. He cut out the shape.
B. He drew a new shape.
C. He put the paint away.
D. He washed the window.

**Answer:** He cut out the shape.

**Explanation:** “Next” introduces cutting, which comes directly before the final painting step.

#### 5. Read: “Asha checked the clock and hurried towards the platform.” Which is a reasonable inference?

A. She has definitely lost her ticket.
B. She owns the station.
C. She is searching for a beach.
D. She may be concerned about missing a train.

**Answer:** She may be concerned about missing a train.

**Explanation:** The clock, hurry and platform suggest a train deadline, but they do not prove that she lost a ticket.

#### 6. Which title best fits a story about neighbours working together to repair a broken garden gate?

A. A Guide to Cloud Shapes
B. My Favourite Sandwich Filling
C. A Gate We Fixed Together
D. The Deepest Ocean

**Answer:** A Gate We Fixed Together

**Explanation:** The chosen title reflects both the gate repair and the neighbours working together.

#### 7. Read: “Eli left a note: ‘I have taken the blue bag. The green one is yours.’” Which bag is available for the reader of the note?

A. Both bags have been taken
B. The green bag
C. The blue bag
D. A red bag

**Answer:** The green bag

**Explanation:** Eli says the green bag belongs to the person reading the note.

#### 8. Read: “Tara felt worried before her first practice. After a friendly welcome, she relaxed.” What changes?

A. Tara feels more comfortable.
B. The practice moves to another city.
C. The welcome becomes unfriendly.
D. Tara becomes more worried.

**Answer:** Tara feels more comfortable.

**Explanation:** The word “relaxed” shows that her earlier worry has eased.

#### 9. Which sentence would clearly tell a reader that a story is moving to an earlier event?

A. The door is beside the window.
B. The door has a round handle.
C. Someone opens the door now.
D. The day before, the same door had been locked.

**Answer:** The day before, the same door had been locked.

**Explanation:** “The day before” signals that the narration is looking back to an earlier time.

#### 10. Read: “Rain stopped the outdoor show, so the musicians played inside the hall.” Which is the best short retelling?

A. The outdoor show continued in the rain.
B. The hall was too small to enter.
C. The musicians moved inside because it rained.
D. The musicians forgot every instrument.

**Answer:** The musicians moved inside because it rained.

**Explanation:** This retelling keeps the cause, the rain, and the main action, moving the performance indoors.

### Multiple-choice questions and answer key

#### 1. A colleague says, “Let us meet next Friday.” You are unsure which date they mean. Which reply checks the meaning?

A. Friday is a good day for me.
B. I will bring the meeting notes.
C. How many people are coming?
D. Do you mean Friday the 12th or Friday the 19th?

**Answer:** Do you mean Friday the 12th or Friday the 19th?

**Explanation:** Giving the two possible dates asks the speaker to resolve the specific uncertainty.

#### 2. You borrowed a neighbour’s ladder and will be late returning it. Which message is most helpful?

A. I will return it at some point.
B. Your ladder is taller than mine.
C. I am sorry for the delay. May I return the ladder tomorrow morning?
D. I borrowed a ladder last week.

**Answer:** I am sorry for the delay. May I return the ladder tomorrow morning?

**Explanation:** The message acknowledges the delay and asks permission for a clear new return time.

#### 3. Choose the sentence that correctly describes an activity happening now.

A. They are set up the chairs now.
B. They are setting up the chairs.
C. They setting up the chairs.
D. They is setting up the chairs.

**Answer:** They are setting up the chairs.

**Explanation:** For an action in progress with “they”, use “are” plus the -ing form: “are setting”.

#### 4. A friend offers to carry your bag. You would like the help. Which response accepts the offer?

A. Yes, please. Thank you for helping.
B. No, thank you. I can manage.
C. Where did you buy your shoes?
D. I disagree with the timetable.

**Answer:** Yes, please. Thank you for helping.

**Explanation:** “Yes, please” clearly accepts the help, and the thanks acknowledge the offer.

#### 5. Which message sets a clear, respectful privacy boundary?

A. My phone number has changed.
B. You can ring me in the afternoon.
C. I spoke to you on the phone yesterday.
D. Please ask me before sharing my phone number.

**Answer:** Please ask me before sharing my phone number.

**Explanation:** The request names the private information and states that permission is needed before sharing it.

#### 6. Read: “The meeting has moved from Room 2 to Room 4. The time is unchanged.” What should you tell a participant?

A. Go to Room 4 on a different day.
B. The meeting has been cancelled.
C. Go to Room 4 at the original time.
D. Go to Room 2 an hour later.

**Answer:** Go to Room 4 at the original time.

**Explanation:** Only the room changes; “the time is unchanged” keeps the original start time.

#### 7. You disagree with a proposed plan. Which reply makes room for discussion?

A. I will change the subject without explaining.
B. I have a concern about the cost. Could we compare another option?
C. Your idea is silly, so stop talking.
D. Every plan is always perfect.

**Answer:** I have a concern about the cost. Could we compare another option?

**Explanation:** This reply identifies a specific concern and invites a useful next step without attacking the person.

#### 8. Read: “Please bring your notebook. You do not need a laptop.” Which reply confirms that you understood?

A. I will bring my notebook and leave my laptop at home.
B. I will bring only my laptop.
C. I will bring neither item.
D. I will ask everyone to bring a laptop.

**Answer:** I will bring my notebook and leave my laptop at home.

**Explanation:** The reply follows both parts of the instruction: bring the notebook and no laptop is required.

#### 9. A caller speaks too quietly for you to hear an address. Which request fits?

A. Could you change your address now?
B. Why are all addresses incorrect?
C. Please send a parcel before I know the address.
D. Could you speak a little louder and repeat the address, please?

**Answer:** Could you speak a little louder and repeat the address, please?

**Explanation:** The request explains the practical need: a louder voice and another reading of the address.

#### 10. Choose the sentence in which “it” clearly refers to the umbrella.

A. I saw the umbrella near the bench, but it was freshly painted.
B. I left the umbrella beside the vase before it fell over.
C. I found the umbrella and put it by the door.
D. I placed the umbrella beside the suitcase, but it was too heavy.

**Answer:** I found the umbrella and put it by the door.

**Explanation:** The umbrella is the clear object being found and then placed by the door.

### Multiple-choice questions and answer key

#### 1. A library survey asked 20 visitors on Tuesday. Sixteen wanted longer opening hours. Which statement matches the evidence?

A. Sixteen of every twenty library members want longer hours.
B. Four fifths of all town residents want longer library hours.
C. Longer hours matter more to Tuesday visitors than to weekend visitors.
D. Sixteen of the visitors surveyed wanted longer hours.

**Answer:** Sixteen of the visitors surveyed wanted longer hours.

**Explanation:** The survey describes those 20 visitors. It does not establish what every member wants or what hours are practical.

#### 2. The notice says: “The new route may reduce journey times.” What does “may” communicate?

A. A record of a benefit already measured.
B. A rule that passengers must arrive early.
C. A possible benefit that is not guaranteed.
D. A promise that every journey will be shorter.

**Answer:** A possible benefit that is not guaranteed.

**Explanation:** “May” marks possibility. The sentence does not report a completed measurement or guarantee a result.

#### 3. A café sold more soup on a cold day than on a warm day. Which conclusion needs more evidence?

A. The two days alone cannot show what happens every day.
B. Cold weather always makes every customer buy soup.
C. The café sold different amounts on the two days.
D. The cold day had higher soup sales in this comparison.

**Answer:** Cold weather always makes every customer buy soup.

**Explanation:** Two daily totals cannot establish an “always” rule or the choice of every individual customer.

#### 4. A review says, “The tiny flat was a palace after the crowded train.” Which reading best fits the comparison?

A. The flat felt spacious or comfortable compared with the train.
B. The flat was owned by a royal family.
C. The train travelled through a palace.
D. The flat became physically larger during the journey.

**Answer:** The flat felt spacious or comfortable compared with the train.

**Explanation:** “A palace” is a figurative comparison here. The phrase describes the traveller’s experience, not a literal change of building.

#### 5. You need tomorrow’s museum opening time. Which source is the most direct place to verify it?

A. A visitor’s blog about a trip six months ago.
B. An undated opening-hours poster shared in a group chat.
C. A guidebook printed before the current year.
D. The museum’s current visitor-information page.

**Answer:** The museum’s current visitor-information page.

**Explanation:** Current information from the organisation responsible for the opening time is the most direct source among these choices.

#### 6. Two explanations fit a higher electricity bill: increased use or a higher tariff. What should a careful comparison check?

A. Only the price per unit, without checking how much was used.
B. Only the final total, without separating use from price.
C. Both the usage figures and the price per unit.
D. Only the number of units used, without checking the tariff.

**Answer:** Both the usage figures and the price per unit.

**Explanation:** Either increased use or a higher price can affect the total. Checking both helps distinguish the explanations.

#### 7. Read: “Mina says the repair is urgent; the inspection report has not arrived.” Which summary preserves who made the judgement?

A. The inspection has proved that Mina is wrong.
B. Mina considers the repair urgent, while the report is still pending.
C. The inspection report confirms that the repair is urgent.
D. Everyone agrees that the repair can wait.

**Answer:** Mina considers the repair urgent, while the report is still pending.

**Explanation:** This attributes the judgement to Mina and keeps the report’s status separate from her opinion.

#### 8. In three repeat measurements, a shelf is 80 cm, 80 cm and 81 cm wide. Which description fits these readings?

A. They are close to one another.
B. They prove the ruler is perfectly accurate.
C. They prove the shelf grew by one centimetre during the checks.
D. They are all exactly the same measurement.

**Answer:** They are close to one another.

**Explanation:** The readings differ by only one centimetre. Agreement alone does not prove that the measuring tool is accurate.

#### 9. A report studies bus stops within one town. Which sentence keeps its conclusion within that scope?

A. All transport systems everywhere need identical signs.
B. No traveller outside the town can read a map.
C. Every town has exactly the same bus-stop problem.
D. Several stops in the town need clearer signs.

**Answer:** Several stops in the town need clearer signs.

**Explanation:** The study concerns one town. Its findings cannot automatically describe every other place.

#### 10. A headline says, “New lamps caused the fall in accidents.” The article reports fewer accidents after installation but checks no other changes. Which revision is more careful?

A. No change in accidents was recorded.
B. The lamps must have had no effect at all.
C. Accidents fell after the lamps were installed; other possible causes still need checking.
D. The lamps have certainly prevented every accident.

**Answer:** Accidents fell after the lamps were installed; other possible causes still need checking.

**Explanation:** The sequence is reported, but the evidence described does not isolate the lamps as the cause or rule out an effect.

### Multiple-choice questions and answer key

#### 1. A hall needs more seating. The floor must be cleared after events, and there is little storage space. Which proposal addresses these needs together?

B. Order the cheapest chairs without checking how they will be stored.

D. Buy folding chairs that can be stored after events.

**Answer:** Buy folding chairs that can be stored after events.

**Explanation:** Folding chairs respond to the need for seats while considering the stated storage limitation.

#### 2. Choose the best connector: “The new printer costs less to buy. ___, its ink is more expensive.”

A. In the same way
B. As a result of this agreement
C. However
D. For example

**Answer:** However

**Explanation:** The second sentence introduces a disadvantage that contrasts with the lower purchase price.

#### 3. A proposal begins, “We should place a water refill point beside the playground.” Which sentence gives a relevant reason?

**Answer:** People using the playground currently walk to another building for drinking water.

#### 4. A team can trial a new queue system at one event before adopting it everywhere. What is the strongest purpose of that trial?

**Answer:** To observe how the system works before making a wider commitment.

**Explanation:** A limited trial provides practical evidence and can reveal adjustments needed before expansion.

#### 5. A garden proposal lists its benefits but gives no information about upkeep. Which question fills that gap?

**Answer:** Who will water and maintain the garden after it opens?

**Explanation:** Long-term maintenance is a practical requirement that the stated benefits do not answer.

#### 6. Read these notes: “Ten residents tried the collection box. Eight found it easy to use. Two could not reach the slot.” Which recommendation uses all the feedback?

**Answer:** Keep the simple design and test a lower slot.

**Explanation:** This preserves a reported strength while responding to the access problem raised by two residents.

#### 7. Which project goal states both a numerical target and a period for checking it?

**Answer:** Reduce paper used for meeting handouts by half this term.

#### 8. A writer recommends a free online form because it is easy to edit. Which sentence fairly acknowledges a limitation?

#### 9. Which order presents the problem first, a proposed solution’s benefit next, and a final recommendation last? A: “A lockable rack would keep the tools together.” B: “Tools are often misplaced after workshops.” C: “I recommend a rack beside the workbench.”

B. A, B, C

**Answer:** B, A, C

**Explanation:** B identifies the problem; A explains what a solution would achieve; C gives the final recommendation. This is the order requested.

#### 10. Two clubs want the same room: one needs quiet practice and the other needs a meeting area. Which response offers a workable compromise?

### Multiple-choice questions and answer key

#### 1. An email asks a colleague to check a revised invoice by Thursday. Which subject line makes its purpose clearest?

A. Revised invoice
B. Invoice approved for payment
C. Thursday meeting arrangements
D. Please review revised invoice by Thursday

**Answer:** Please review revised invoice by Thursday

**Explanation:** The subject identifies the action, the document and the deadline, helping the reader judge what is needed.

#### 2. A notice says, “Return the form by 12:00 on 18 June.” Which rewrite keeps the deadline clear?

A. Submit the form after noon on 18 June.
B. Submit the form by noon on 19 June.
C. Submit the form no later than noon on 18 June.
D. Submit the form by midnight on 18 June.

**Answer:** Submit the form no later than noon on 18 June.

**Explanation:** Noon is 12:00. The rewrite retains both the time and the date.

#### 3. A customer reports a damaged item. You need the order number before investigating. Which reply clearly and courteously asks for the detail you need?

C. I’m sorry the item arrived damaged. We will close this request now.
D. Please send your passport and bank statement so we can locate the order.

**Answer:** I’m sorry the item arrived damaged. Please send your order number so we can check the delivery.

**Explanation:** The reply acknowledges the problem, asks for a relevant detail and explains why it is needed.

#### 4. An instruction reads, “Utilise the designated receptacle for the disposal of paper.” Which plain-language version keeps the meaning?

A. Put paper in the marked bin.
B. Put all rubbish in the marked bin.
C. Place paper beside the marked bin.
D. Put paper in any available bin.

**Answer:** Put paper in the marked bin.

**Explanation:** This keeps the action, material and specified destination while replacing unnecessarily formal wording.

**Answer:** Thank you; I’ve received your request and will send a full reply tomorrow.

**Explanation:** It confirms receipt and gives a specific next step without pretending the issue is already resolved.

#### 6. A draft message says, “We need it soon.” Which replacement specifies both the document and a complete deadline?

**Explanation:** It specifies what to send and an exact deadline, replacing the unclear “it” and “soon”.
