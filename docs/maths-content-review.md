## Scope and learning principles

The proposed scope spans early numeracy, school mathematics, practical adult numeracy and major university/advanced mathematical branches. The specialist units introduce their named fields and can expand into deeper course libraries; a topic map alone is not a claim that every research subfield or full graduate course has been authored.

Levels describe current knowledge, not age or eligibility. Children, teenagers and adults can choose any starting point. Prerequisites are recommendations, and optional placement questions suggest a starting point without locking content. University and advanced units branch by prerequisites rather than requiring every learner to complete a single long ladder.

The target is 200 topic units with 40 reviewed core problems each: 8,000 core problems. A unit contains several small lessons and practice rounds. The counts below are a proposed authoring target, not questions already built. Additional validated numerical/diagram variants provide further practice and are counted separately. New problem contexts, reasoning and common misconceptions should be represented, not just reordered choices.

Approximately 67% of the proposed core bank is MCQ. Foundation items usually use two or three clear choices; later items normally use four. Distractors reflect identifiable mistakes and every single-choice item has one defensible correct answer under its stated assumptions.

## Learner experience

- All lessons, puzzles, placement and challenges are untimed. No countdowns, deadlines, speed bonuses or speed rankings. Clocks and elapsed time may appear as mathematical topics, never as an activity timer.
- Practice is offered in five-question rounds. More rounds are always available; a learner may pause, save and resume an unfinished round.
- Each unit has Learn, Explore, Practise and Review: a clear idea, worked examples, an interactive model, practice, then answers with explanations.
- Within a topic, choose Gentle, Core or Stretch difficulty. These adjust the number of steps, abstraction and reasoning, with age-neutral wording.
- On a mistake, offer a useful hint, another attempt and a worked solution. A revealed solution is recorded as assisted practice, not an independently correct answer.
- Review shows the learner's response, the correct answer, steps, a common-mistake explanation and a related follow-up question.
- Completion, accuracy and independent practice remain separate measures. Reward completed learning and improvement; repeated identical work does not repeatedly award full XP.
- Completed Maths practice contributes to the existing learning streak with two fully missed days of grace. Previously earned badges and XP remain safe.
- Learners can choose playful or simple presentation. Tap/keyboard alternatives accompany drag interactions. Equations, diagrams and controls need accessible labels and colour-independent feedback. Optional audio, celebrations and motion respect the learner's preferences.
- Scientific tools are available where appropriate. The question states whether it is practising mental/written calculation, exact mathematics or a numerical approximation. An always-available scratch area supports working.

## Answer and explanation rules

1. Every published item needs an objective, prerequisites, difficulty, prompt, stated assumptions, answer key, useful hint, worked explanation and accepted-response rules.
2. Equivalent values are accepted when the requested format allows them. For example, 1/2 and 0.5 can both be valid for a numerical value; a request for a fraction still requires fraction form.
3. State units and whether a number, a quantity with units, an interval, a set or a vector is required. Dimensionally incorrect units receive specific feedback. Ordered coordinates and matrix dimensions matter.
4. State exact versus approximate answers. Exact π or radical answers should not be rejected in favour of rounded decimals when exact form is requested. When approximation is requested, state the rounding precision and use an appropriate numerical tolerance.
5. State real/complex domains, degree/radian conventions and restrictions. For real x, √(x²) = |x|; simplification must not discard domain conditions.
6. Accept appropriate equivalent algebraic forms under the stated domain using constrained, validated checks. Expressions that cannot be assessed confidently need a supported structured answer or model-solution review, not a confident wrong verdict.
7. Indefinite integrals require an arbitrary constant when a general antiderivative is requested. Probability problems specify relevant sampling, independence and conditioning assumptions.
8. A full proof can have many valid approaches. The learner sees a model argument and a checklist; free-form proof quality is not reduced to exact string matching or an unchecked AI judgment.
9. Numeric/diagram variants must preserve solvability, domains, nonzero denominators, valid shapes, intended difficulty and a correct answer. MCQ options must remain distinct after simplification and rounding.
10. Check the mathematics independently before release, including boundary cases and accepted equivalent responses. Later changes to a question must preserve earlier results or explicitly version the content.

### Sample round 11 — Advanced Pure Maths

**1. Do the functions fₙ(x) = xⁿ converge uniformly on [0,1]?**

Response: MCQ.

- A. Yes; the limit is 0 everywhere on [0,1].
- B. No; the pointwise limit is discontinuous at 1.
- C. Yes; every fₙ is continuous.
- D. No; the sequence has no pointwise limit.

**Correct answer:** No; the pointwise limit is discontinuous at 1.

**Worked explanation:** The pointwise limit is 0 for 0 ≤ x < 1 and 1 at x = 1. A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, so convergence is not uniform on [0,1].

**2. If X is compact and f:X→Y is continuous, which conclusion always holds?**

Response: MCQ.

- A. f(X) is compact.
- B. f(X) is open.
- C. f is injective.
- D. f is constant.

**Correct answer:** f(X) is compact.

**Worked explanation:** Pull an open cover of f(X) back through f to an open cover of X. Compactness supplies a finite subcover, and its corresponding sets cover f(X).

**3. Is A₃ a normal subgroup of S₃?**

Response: MCQ.

- A. Yes; it has index 2.
- B. No; S₃ is not abelian.
- C. Only if S₃ is cyclic.
- D. No; A₃ has three elements.

**Correct answer:** Yes; it has index 2.

**Worked explanation:** A₃ has order 3 and S₃ has order 6, so its index is 2. Every index-2 subgroup is normal; equivalently, A₃ is the kernel of the sign homomorphism.

**4. Enter the multiplicative inverse of 3 modulo 7 as an integer from 0 to 6.**

Response: Typed residue.

**Correct answer:** 5

**Worked explanation:** 3 × 5 = 15 ≡ 1 modulo 7. The requested representative is 5.

**5. Find the residue of e^z/z² at z = 0.**

Response: Typed complex-analysis answer.

**Correct answer:** 1

**Worked explanation:** e^z = 1 + z + z²/2 + … . Dividing by z² gives z⁻² + z⁻¹ + 1/2 + … . The coefficient of z⁻¹ is 1.

### Sample round 12 — Advanced Applied Maths

**1. Which value of x minimises (x − 3)² subject to x ≥ 5?**

Response: MCQ.

- A. 3
- B. 4
- C. 5
- D. 8

**Correct answer:** 5

**Worked explanation:** The unconstrained minimiser 3 is infeasible. On x ≥ 5 the function increases, so the constrained minimiser is the boundary x = 5. The minimum function value is 4.

**2. 10% of factory items are defective. A sensor flags 90% of defective items and 10% of non-defective items. Given that an item is flagged, what is the probability it is defective?**

Response: MCQ.

- A. 10%
- B. 50%
- C. 90%
- D. 100%

**Correct answer:** 50%

**Worked explanation:** P(defective and flagged) = 0.1 × 0.9 = 0.09. P(flagged) = 0.09 + 0.9 × 0.1 = 0.18. The conditional probability is 0.09/0.18 = 1/2.

**3. Use gradient descent on f(x) = x² with x₀ = 4 and step size 0.1. What is x₁?**

Response: MCQ.

- A. 0.8
- B. 3.2
- C. 3.6
- D. 4.8

**Correct answer:** 3.2

**Worked explanation:** f′(4) = 8. The update is x₁ = 4 − 0.1 × 8 = 3.2.

**4. Apply one Newton step to f(x) = x² − 2 starting at x₀ = 1. Find x₁.**

Response: Typed exact or decimal answer.

**Correct answer:** 3/2 or 1.5

**Worked explanation:** x₁ = x₀ − f(x₀)/f′(x₀) = 1 − (−1)/2 = 3/2.

**5. For a row-vector Markov model with transition-matrix rows (0.8,0.2) and (0.4,0.6), find the stationary distribution π = (π₁,π₂).**

Response: Typed vector.

**Correct answer:** (2/3, 1/3)

**Worked explanation:** Solve πP = π and π₁ + π₂ = 1. The first equation gives 0.2π₁ = 0.4π₂, so π₁ = 2π₂. Normalisation gives (2/3,1/3).
